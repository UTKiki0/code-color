## Ideation

### Ideas

The idea brought to this discussion was a painting-themed match-3 battle RPG. Exploration focused on variations of that concept rather than unrelated projects:

- **Color abilities and equipment builds:** Match paint bottles, store colors, and spend them on abilities. Brushes and rollers change attack styles. This appeals to the Developer because it combines approachable matching with RPG build choices. The main concern is balancing interacting paint, mixing, and equipment systems.
- **Painting-puzzle battles:** Complete enemy canvases using required colors or patterns. This was suggested as an alternative but was not selected; the discussion developed combat through color abilities instead.
- **Handcrafted progression or repeatable runs:** Handcrafted battles would offer control over teaching and pacing. The Developer chose repeatable runs for equipment variety and character unlocks; this requires enough encounter variety to sustain replay.

The selected audience is RPG players who enjoy builds, while keeping the core rules approachable for casual players. Broader exploration of several distinct project ideas, as requested by the brief, has not yet been completed.

### Choice

The Developer chose **Code: Color**, a match-3 battle RPG with repeatable runs. The motivation is to make matching and winning feel fair and approachable, informed by the Developer's frustration with perceived pressure to buy microtransactions in other match-3 games.

The chosen structure uses separate palette mixing, enemy countdowns that advance only through matching, equipment found within runs, and permanent character unlocks. Handcrafted campaign progression was set aside. Equipment and paint do not persist between runs.

### Branding

Suggested titles included Hue Havoc, Palette Panic, and Brush Brawl. The Developer supplied and chose **Code: Color**.

The logo combines two treatments: **Code:** looks like a secret mission, while **Color** appears as a messy blotch of paint. The game's overall tone is playful, slightly chaotic, and cartoony. Exact typography, colors, and the finished logo remain to be developed.

## Pitch

Draft for Developer review; Studio director approval is required before development. The phase map and validation checks below are proposals for review.

### Concept

**Code: Color** is a painting-themed match-3 battle RPG built around repeatable runs targeting roughly 30 minutes. Players match bottles to collect paint, mix stored colors on a separate palette, and spend paint to fight enemy canvases. Brushes and rollers change attack styles, encouraging players to experiment with builds.

Each successful board move advances enemy countdowns once, including all cascades from that move. Enemies attack when their countdown reaches zero. Spending and mixing paint do not advance countdowns, giving players time to consider their choices.

### Title and branding

The working title is **Code: Color**. The logo contrasts a secret-mission treatment for “Code:” with “Color” rendered as a messy paint blotch. Playful, slightly chaotic cartoon visuals connect the bottles, equipment, and enemy canvases. The logo is a direction rather than a completed asset; typography, palette, and visual rules will be developed and recorded in `spec.md`.

### Why this project

The Developer feels that microtransaction pressure can make match-3 games frustrating to play and win. This project aims for fair, understandable challenge and satisfying victories without purchase pressure. This is the Developer's motivation, not a researched claim about the genre as a whole.

Combining matching, resource decisions, and equipment builds provides a substantial design and implementation challenge. The project also creates an opportunity to learn Godot more deeply while building a complete game.

### Users

The primary audience is RPG players who enjoy experimenting with builds. Casual players should also be able to understand the matching, paint abilities, and mixing rules without needing to learn a large set of systems at once. Both groups need clear feedback about available paint, equipment effects, and incoming enemy attacks.

### Experience goals

Players should feel playful experimentation and manageable tension as they gather paint and watch enemy countdowns. They should be able to plan between board moves, understand what their colors and tools do, and see how equipment changes their approach.

Colors have distinct effects, such as red causing fire and blue slowing enemies. Mixing uses familiar paint recipes; the exact recipes, costs, and effects remain open. The goal is approachable challenge with room for thoughtful builds.

### Technical goals

Build a match-3 board that resolves matches and cascades, stores collected paint, and advances enemy countdowns exactly once per successful move. Connect it to palette mixing, paint abilities, equipment effects, and battle outcomes.

Support runs with battles, equipment rewards, and a final boss. Scope three characters: one initially available and two unlocked through distinct goals. Possible goals include a boss victory and an overall run score; exact conditions and scoring remain undecided. Characters have different starting equipment. Acquired equipment and stored paint reset after each run; unlocked characters remain available.

### Starting point

The game will be built from scratch. External libraries, assets, and references used during development will be documented with sources, usage terms, and required credits in `research.md`.

### Tech stack

Use **Godot**. The Developer has used both Godot and Unity, but has less experience with Godot and wants to learn it further. The scripting language, engine version, and public delivery platform remain to be selected. The finished game must be publicly usable online or in person.

### MVP or critical function

Prove one playable battle before expanding into full runs. It must demonstrate bottle matching, cascades, paint storage, separate palette mixing, paint abilities, an equipment effect, and enemy countdowns.

Proposed checks: one move with a cascade collects the correct paint but advances countdowns only once; mixing and spending paint leave countdowns unchanged; an enemy attacks at zero; the player can win or lose and restart. This validates the core interaction before adding run content and permanent unlocks.

No stretch features are approved yet. Additional characters, equipment, and encounters may be considered after the agreed scope works; their priority requires Developer review.

### Risks

- **Learning Godot:** Unfamiliar workflows could slow development. Use the first battle to expose implementation difficulties early.
- **Interacting systems:** Mixing, equipment, and abilities could become difficult to teach or balance. Test a small set together before expanding it.
- **Countdown balance:** Free paint spending or slowing effects could remove enemy pressure. Test whether finite paint and the need to match again preserve meaningful decisions; exact rules remain open.
- **Run length and content:** Thirty minutes could demand more variety than the schedule permits. Test a short complete run first and review pacing before adding content.
- **Fairness:** Difficulty and random boards could undermine the intended approachable experience. Observe playtests and revise based on where players struggle.
- **Delivery and persistence:** Public delivery and permanent character unlocks need verification on the chosen platform early enough to address failures.
